# The Rework — 12 September 2026

Greg's integral rework, transcribed from an eighteen-page document and organised
without being rewritten. His words are quoted wherever they carry the idea
better than a summary would, which is most of the time.

**This is the most consequential document in the project so far**, because it is
the first one that answers *what the game is about* rather than what it does.
Four of the five questions that have been formally blocked on Greg in
`CHECKLIST.md` are answered here.

---

## 1. What happened to the world

The setting was never written down. It is now.

> *"post ai in an age post industrial revolutions collapse and the destruction
> of the earth, the sky / firmament, the countries, embassies, food, war"*

**The nuclear war is fake.** This is the load-bearing fact of the whole setting,
and it is exactly the right one for a game whose central rule is already *two
records: what happened, and what each faction believes*.

> *"'nuclear' unknown testing facilities on site that produce such high levels of
> radiation, they have been theorised to have been fake nuclear attacks as no
> footage exists although gore and crisis actors news segments"*

**What actually fell it was an alien apocalypse** — an attack on what Earth
really is:

> *"the world fell when an alien apocalypse happened as an attack on the industry
> and prison planet by a freed colony, although many theorise its the government
> still living in the deep tunnel networks"*

So: Earth is an **industry and prison planet**. Somebody who got out came back
for it. And the official story is a lie told by people who are still alive
underneath the place they lied about.

The state of things now:

- Sparse. Humans are **post-AI, post-capitalism, and simultaneously stone-age
  barbaric** — the collapse did not distribute evenly.
- **The elites are the exception.** They have the Wire, they have the nemesis
  system, they cannot really die in the traditional sense.
- Cities and ruins still stand. **The sky is broken**, and *"the gods for each
  planet and moon and sun become visible at certain times"*.
- **The seals broke when the world ended.** Every demon is now observable, and
  every sigil is live again — *"but captureable"*.
- Villages, cities and bandit camps **fight back aggressively**. This is not a
  world waiting to be looted.

### The tunnels

> *"the government still living in the deep tunnel networks that are urbex,
> overgrown, Outlast-esque, covered in blood, gore, horrors, as the military have
> gone cannibalistic and blood thirsty with greed of power, slowly dismantling
> themselves as you play, as the authority in the game is no help"*

The detail that makes it work: **they are dismantling themselves while you
play.** The tunnels are not a static horror level, they are an institution
eating itself on a clock.

## 2. Who you are

> *"you start as a captured spirit in the government facility underground, to be
> meat for the elites and scrap"*

And the thing that makes you a problem for them:

> *"they see the essence of his spirit as it can't be banished by torturing,
> killing, violence — it stays in pure bright flames that are vibrant and melt
> the game's screen... the character's spirit is undying, so the government wants
> to enslave you"*

**You are not powerful. You are unkillable, which is worse for everyone.** That
is a much better protagonist premise than a strong one, and it explains why an
institution that could simply shoot you instead spends the game trying to own
you.

- **The opening quiz is real.** *"although you are being tested, the quiz can
  matter at the start — I want to be able to balance but make it matter in a
  mystical way."*
- **You are the progression.** *"I'm not sure if I want you soulless and your
  soul continuing down the game's line of progression, as you are the
  progression and the ending."*
- The register: *"like Tell-Tale sort of, but more psychology tested using intense
  scripts to make decisions matter, like the game Life is Strange."*
- *"through the abundance of your own presence the story guides itself"*
- **Blood, rust and scrap have joined in alchemy**, and they hide a secret
  element you work out later.

### Wizard eyes

> *"your spirit kinda just guides you based around blood, the stars, and guides
> you through 'wizard eyes'"*

Adventure Time's Ice King, as a mechanic: usable every so often, highlighting
*"insane spirits, ghosts, phantoms — but not too insane, pretty abstract art
based off using clips of different cuts, collaging my old and current art to use
as textures intelligently."*

The finger symbol from Adventure Time is in the art collections folder and
**becomes the logo, designed as a sigil**.

## 3. What you are fighting

> *"the one true godhead, being commenting and enslaving us all in its own
> lessons and learning, is the fightable true end game boss"*

Everything about how it is introduced is right:

- **It taunts you**, and *"slowly builds up visibility and what it does and
  says"*. It is not revealed; it accumulates.
- **It summons you rather than being reached.** *"when that godhead contacts you
  or summons your conscious spirit in the shadow realms of the higher realms"* —
  the DMT and psychedelic register, *"as well as traits, emotions, real gods,
  demons, current folklore with esoteric conspiracy knowledge"*.
- It is not evil in the ordinary way. It enslaves **through its own lessons and
  learning** — which is the most interesting possible thing for a final boss in
  a game about institutions, and it is one short step from AJ3's modern gods.

## 4. The paths

Not a morality slider. A **diagram**.

> *"it be a big Kabbalah type tree of life or Nordic tree of life diagram for the
> different paths, when they occur in the canon story beats, chapters"*

This sits directly alongside AI's double pyramid: the pyramid is *where power
is*, the tree is *which way you went*. Two charts, one document.

The paths named so far:

| Path | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| **The common CellOutz demon** | *"you can, but you have to have aura or a significant power or influence"* |
| **Rebel / outcast from the gods** | Refuse both ladders. Everything gets harder and nobody owns you |
| **Low-frequency demons** | Then you can side with *"the Peter Thiel-esque class, Epstein class, reptilian classes"* |
| **Turn the demons on God** | *"or you can make the demons fight them, and make the demons challenge God"* |

And the job market underneath all of it:

> *"there is an economy with jobs in the world... if you're a bounty seeker for
> the top angels or top demons, and hunt the evil doers who help prevent certain
> demons' or angels' frequencies, auras"*

Plus **Chainsaw Man-style contracts**: consumable, and *"consumable progress with
bosses and big figures of progress"*.

## 5. The opening

A real sequence, in order, for the first time:

1. **Captured spirit**, government facility, underground. Meat and scrap.
2. **The quiz.** It matters, mystically.
3. **Experimented on and tortured** — *"you find out throughout the game, so you
   don't spend heaps of time at the start faffing"*.
4. **The gore festival.** *"genuine slabs of pressed meat."*
5. **The underground tunnel derby** — reptilians, aliens, *"evil elite rich
   bunker AI dwellers who are the true face of the evils, as you have to execute
   them all"*.
6. **Out.** *"you can choose different paths of freedom for the lands."*

**This makes the derby story-critical rather than a side mode.** It is the thing
you fight your way out of, and the Bone Yard as it exists today is a version of
it that the fiction has not caught up with yet.

Greg's pacing rule for all of it: *"keep game super digestible segment to segment,
but also a possible playground if you want."*

## 6. Mechanics the rework adds

### Night, light and the phone
> *"the light can become really warped at night and distorted. Phone has a %
> possibly, or just really minimal lighting, and you can wave it around showing
> lighting — as well as having light coming off the phone when you have it in
> your hand, then you can wave it around or pocket it."*

- The handheld is a **light source**, and holding it up is a real action.
- It has a **battery percentage**.
- **Pockets and clothes are integral** — *"or at least a part of the world system,
  layers and strategy to everything"*.

### Weather that answers the occult
> *"I also want the weather to have consistent crazy storms depending on spirits
> levels, chaos magick levels... the lightning in the game needs to have anvil
> crawlers, all the crazy red lighting-esque things."*

Weather is not ambience. It is a **readout of how much magick is loose**.

### The black mirror
- **Evoke entities** with certain information; gain power and knowledge.
- **Challenge their souls** — free, enslave, or *"kill and revive into
  suffering"*.
- **The Akashic records library.**
- **A social media app** where *"doom scroll spammers and drone zombie mutants
  dying on the internet documenting"* can be cleared and killed *"to help clean
  up feeds of local areas for money or requests"*.
- The Wire holds **playable apps** — *"some for fun, some actually integral to
  gameplay, same as arcades in the map"*.

### The body
- Everything **inspectable**, full inventory, *"with cybernetics and organs and
  bones visible"*.
- **Limbs that shoot missiles and grapple** — *"like Mirror's Edge, Prototype,
  Spider-Man mix of wall running"*.
- **Crystal ball** in the arm or the pocket.
- Head mutations, piercings, body mods, tattoos, extensions.
- **Radiation** — *"there's like 9g or 8g that radiation really melts you"*.

### The weapon menu
> *"the slide pops out into a menu if you press shift and lock it, expanding it
> into the weapon customisation — maybe the circle in the middle and 4 boxes or
> something around it."*

### Endgame
> *"quantum immortality deaths or restarts in other universes — but that's a new
> game + end game feature. Still a good idea to explain different canon story
> beats."*

**This answers T1.1** — what persists between runs — and answers it better than
a save-file rule would: *the universe changes, you don't*.

## 9. The one line to hold

Everything above is a world where an institution lied about the end of the world,
is eating itself underground, and cannot kill the one thing it captured.

The satire lands on **institutions** — the agency, the bank, the military, the
elite class, the godhead that teaches you into obedience. It never lands on
congregations, on believers, or on any group of actual people. Greg has already
drawn this line himself once today: *"not as much qanon shit, but just black
magick and chaos magick."* Same line, and it holds here.
